Bitcoin was US$57,700 and US$16,900 on the same exchange

On 21 November 2026 at 08:00:43 UTC, Kraken's BTC/CAD market traded at C$24,250 — US$16,928 at the Bank of Canada rate — three seconds after Kraken's BTC/USD traded at US$57,734. It was not a bitcoin crash: every Canadian-dollar market on Kraken moved together, while the US-dollar markets stayed near global prices. We rebuilt the event from every executed trade in Kraken's public data. Below: what happened, how we checked it, what it cannot tell us, and what WATAM did and did not see.

The selling pattern: execution clusters

Kraken's public trade records carry no order id. We group market-sell trades whose timestamps fall within 5 ms of each other into execution clusters; a cluster is most likely one taker order, but the data does not establish that. On BTC/CAD, 29 execution clusters of 5 BTC or more account for 371.25 of the window's 374.86 BTC of market sells:

  • 9 clusters at :04:34–:04:35 past each hour (20 Nov 19:04 → 21 Nov 03:04), 8 of exactly 20.8333 BTC — 182.49 BTC;
  • 18 clusters every 15 minutes at :43 seconds (21 Nov 05:15 → 09:30), 10 of exactly 10.4167 BTC — 171.08 BTC; the others executed smaller sizes;
  • 2 more: 8.3333 BTC at 18:27:02 and 9.3484 BTC at 04:27:01.

Equal sizes on a fixed clock are consistent with a scheduled execution. The public data does not show whether a cluster is one order, who sent these trades, whether they came from one account, why, or why some clusters executed below the recurring size. Kraken's REST documentation lists the side and order-type fields without further definition; we read "s" + "m" as a market sell.

What Kraken's market price protection measures

Kraken's Market Price Protection cancels a market order "as soon as the bid/ask spread gets unusually wide" (BTC/CAD: 15%). It looks at the spread of the pair's own book — not at BTC/USD or an external FX rate — so a CAD book whose bids and asks move down together can stay inside it. Which orders it cancelled during the event is not visible in public data. Kraken said on 21 Nov, as quoted by Finance Magnates, that its platform "processed every order correctly", found no breach, and that "executed trades stand".

What WATAM saw — and what we changed

  • Saw: Kraken's BTC/USDT book every 15 minutes (72 of 72 samples in the window; mid US$57,820.5 at 08:04 UTC).
  • Missed: Kraken's CAD books were not in our samplers (they read USD-stable, TRY and KRW books), and no job compared prices across fiat quotes. We have no live record of the CAD books; nothing on this page was reconstructed as if we had.
  • Under validation (not live): Kraken CAD books every 15 minutes, converted at the independent Bank of Canada daily rate — not at the venue's own USD/CAD, which itself traded down to 0.30 — and an operator alert when a CAD book and the same venue's USD book disagree by 5% or more. We tested it by replaying the executed-trade prices of 20–21 Nov at the sampler's read times; a trade replay is not order-book data, so it does not show when live monitoring would have alerted.

Reproduce it

  1. fetch_trades.py downloads the trades from Kraken's public API into data/ (about 1 request per 1.2 s; it skips the trade the since cursor repeats at each page boundary). Its header lists the two commands used.
  2. Save https://api.kraken.com/0/public/OHLC?pair=XXBTZCAD&interval=15 and the same for XBTUSDT as data/ohlc15_<pair>.json. That endpoint keeps only the latest 720 candles (7.5 days); after 28 Nov 2026 use Kraken's downloadable OHLCVT archive.
  3. evidence.py recomputes every figure on this page into evidence.json.

Bank of Canada rate: Valet FXUSDCAD. No Kraken data is hosted here.

What this does and does not say

  • It says what executed, at what price and time, on which Kraken markets, and that the move was confined to the CAD quote.
  • It does not identify any trader, attribute intent, or say that any rule or system failed. Order-book states between trades and internal order handling are not public.
  • USD equivalents use one daily reference rate; a few tenths of a percent of ordinary FX movement are within that rounding.
