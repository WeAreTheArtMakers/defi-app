The 29 Oct “Binance 14” outflow on DEX pools, block by block

On 29 Oct 2026 the wallet labelled “Binance 14” on Etherscan sent ETH, stablecoins and 60+ tokens to a fresh address. We did not look at who or why. We looked at what happened next in the DEX pools of the tokens that left it — every number below links to the block or transaction it comes from.

What happened

  • At 20:24:23 UTC on 29 Oct (block 26,300,770, tx 0x53bd38b8…), the wallet labelled “Binance 14” (0x28c6…1d60) sent ETH, stablecoins and 63 token transfers to a fresh address, 0x5227…2f8c — “the receiver” below. More transfers to it followed in the next blocks.
  • As of 31 Oct 2026 we have not found a formal statement from Binance explaining this outflow. Press reports quote people and figures we have not verified, so we leave them out. We make no claim about the cause.
  • Stablecoins can be frozen by their issuers, and both did: on-chain at block 26,315,134, the receiver is blacklisted by USDT (isBlackListed = true) and by USDC (isBlacklisted = true), with 1,175,464,712.88 USDT and 823,000,000 USDC still on it.
  • Most other tokens cannot be frozen. 14 of the tokens in the outflow are in our catalog. Between 21:51 on 29 Oct and 01:56 UTC on 30 Oct, 13 of the 14 were sold into Uniswap pools, either by fresh addresses that had received them from the receiver or by the receiver itself. SNX (8,405,742) still sits at the small contract it was moved to.

ONDO: one seller, one pool, −91% inside one block

The receiver sent 21,851,871 ONDO to 0x42cd…7810 (block 26,301,515, tx 0x6bf04dd8…). That address sold all of it into one pool, Uniswap v3 ONDO/WETH 0.3% (0x7b1e…3cc7), in 29 sales between blocks 26,301,556 and 26,301,642 (23:01–23:19 UTC) — about one sale every three blocks.

Each sale pushed the pool price down, by 8% to 91% inside its block. Other swaps in the same block, most likely arbitrage, bought ONDO back, so at the end of every block the price was at most about 20% below where it started. The deepest sale was in block 26,301,597 (tx 0x03fcad03…): −90.8%. From the first sale to block 26,301,760, about 72% of all ONDO sold into this pool came from this one address.

UNI: 7.02M sold across eight venues

The same address 0x42cd…7810 had received 7,018,792 UNI earlier (block 26,301,181, tx 0x028bdd8b…) — more than the 6.19M in the first transfer, because of later top-ups. It sold all of it in 42 transactions between blocks 26,301,203 and 26,301,460 (21:51–22:42 UTC), through aggregators and solvers.

In the main pool, Uniswap v3 UNI/WETH 0.3% (0x1d42…d801), the lowest price inside a block was −40% (block 26,301,440, tx 0x1ab63674…). Unlike ONDO, the end-of-block price also drifted lower, to about −14%, while the wider market was falling at the same time. By block 26,301,760 it was back to −1%.

Our monitor caught it as it happened

Our near-real-time large-trade monitor (part of Live Pro) raised 99 alerts on Ethereum for these tokens between blocks 26,301,184 and 26,301,696 (21:47–23:30 UTC). Afterwards we matched each alert’s transaction against the addresses on this page. The alert list comes from our internal alert history; the transactions behind each alert are public.

  • 23 were the sells by outflow-linked addresses themselves: UNI 8, AAVE 5, ONDO 4, ENA 3, LINK 2, WLD 1.
  • 20 were reaction trades right after them, most likely arbitrage or buybacks (our reading of the flow); 4 were volume bursts that overlap the sells.
  • 52 were market-wide moves with no link we could find — including every PENDLE, LDO, ETHFI and COMP alert. Those tokens had not left the receiver yet at that time.

How to check it yourself

Everything above comes from public Ethereum logs. Here is the ONDO finding, end to end. One request returns every swap in the ONDO/WETH 0.3% pool from block 26,301,550 to 26,301,650:

curl -s -X POST https://gateway.tenderly.co/public/mainnet -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{
  "jsonrpc": "2.0", "id": 1, "method": "eth_getLogs",
  "params": [{
    "address": "0x7b1e5d984a43ee732de195628d20d05cfabc3cc7",
    "topics": ["0xc42079f94a6350d7e6235f29174924f928cc2ac818eb64fed8004e115fbcca67"],
    "fromBlock": "0x191546e", "toBlock": "0x19154d2"
  }]}'

Some free nodes only keep the most recent logs (publicnode answers “archive request” for these blocks); on 31 Oct 2026 Tenderly’s public gateway and https://rpc.mevblocker.io both returned them. The script below adds the sender of each swap’s transaction and the pool price. It printed exactly what this page says:

import json, urllib.request
RPC = "https://gateway.tenderly.co/public/mainnet"   # any Ethereum node that still serves logs from 29 Oct 2026
POOL = "0x7b1e5d984a43ee732de195628d20d05cfabc3cc7"   # Uniswap v3 ONDO/WETH 0.3%: token0 = WETH, token1 = ONDO (18 decimals each)
SWAP = "0xc42079f94a6350d7e6235f29174924f928cc2ac818eb64fed8004e115fbcca67"
SELLER = "0x42cdf00c85005630cc8605731b9364f712077810"
def call(method, params):
    req = urllib.request.Request(RPC, json.dumps({"jsonrpc": "2.0", "id": 1, "method": method, "params": params}).encode(),
                                 {"Content-Type": "application/json", "User-Agent": "check"})
    return json.load(urllib.request.urlopen(req))["result"]
def word(log, i):
    v = int(log["data"][2 + 64 * i:2 + 64 * (i + 1)], 16)
    return v - 2**256 if v >= 2**255 else v
logs = call("eth_getLogs", [{"address": POOL, "topics": [SWAP], "fromBlock": hex(26301550), "toBlock": hex(26301650)}])
price = lambda log: 1 / (word(log, 2) / 2**96) ** 2      # WETH per ONDO after the swap (sqrtPriceX96 is word 2)
senders = {h: call("eth_getTransactionByHash", [h])["from"].lower() for h in {l["transactionHash"] for l in logs}}
before, sold, sells = None, 0.0, 0
for log in logs:
    if senders[log["transactionHash"]] == SELLER and word(log, 1) > 0:
        sold += word(log, 1) / 1e18; sells += 1               # amount1 > 0: ONDO went into the pool
    elif sells == 0:
        before = log                                          # last swap before the first sale
low = min(logs, key=price)
print(f"{len(logs)} swaps; {sells} sales by the seller, {sold:,.2f} ONDO")
print(f"before: {price(before):.8f} WETH/ONDO; low: {price(low):.8f} in block {int(low['blockNumber'], 16)} "
      f"({100 * (price(low) / price(before) - 1):.1f}%), tx {low['transactionHash']}")
# 438 swaps; 29 sales by the seller, 21,851,870.65 ONDO
# before: 0.00016547 WETH/ONDO; low: 0.00001517 in block 26301597 (-90.8%), tx 0x03fcad03…bc6c

The same method — pool Swap logs, sqrtPriceX96, and the sender of each transaction — gives every other number on this page. Token amounts that left an address come from its ERC-20 Transfer logs; the blacklist flags come from isBlackListed (USDT) and isBlacklisted (USDC) calls.

What this does and does not say

  • It shows transfers and swaps that anyone can see on Ethereum. It does not say who controls these addresses, why the outflow happened, or whose funds they were. We make no attribution.
  • “Outflow-linked” is our label for a transaction sent by, or moving tokens from, an address that received tokens from the receiver (list below). A seller we have not identified would count as market activity.
  • The whole market fell on the evening of 29 Oct. Price drift at the end of a block (for example UNI at −14%) cannot be pinned on these sales alone. The lows inside a block can, because they are the pool’s state right after a specific sale.
  • Lows are pool prices inside one block. A thin pool can be pushed to near zero by one large sale; that is not a market price and not what any trader necessarily paid.
  • Shares such as “about 72% of ONDO sold into the pool” are approximate: they depend on the block window and on what counts as a sale.
  • Ethereum only; other chains were not checked. No USD values: all amounts are token units.
