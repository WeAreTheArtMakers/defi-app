Same token, different order books: SAPIEN on 12 exchanges, 17–25 Nov 2026

In the week Bitcoin fell below $60,000, we were sampling the public order books of one mid-cap token, SAPIEN, on 12 exchanges every five minutes. Four of those books carried less than half of their earlier depth for four days, then returned to their earlier level, three of them between two consecutive samples. The other books moved far less. Below are the observations, how we checked them, and what they do not say.

What we measured

  • Metric: the weaker side (the smaller of bid and ask) of each public order book within ±2% of its mid price, in USD (USDT counted at $1). One sample per book every 5 minutes, at the same moment on every exchange.
  • Books: 12 exchanges, 14 books (Binance and MEXC also have USDC pairs, which are not used here). Window medians are taken per book over every 5-minute sample in the window; adding medians of different books gives a sum of medians, not a total at one moment.
  • Windows: before = 17–19 Nov; during = 20 Nov 08:00 – 24 Nov 09:00 UTC; after = 24 Nov 10:00 – 25 Nov 04:00 UTC.

How we checked it

  • Prices: our hourly mids match the official hourly candles of Binance (SAPIENUSDT) and Coinbase (SAPIEN-USD) for 17–25 Nov: median difference 0.24% and 0.28% over 192 hours.
  • Today's books: on 25 Nov at 03:38 UTC, Binance's public API gave ±2% bid $54,097 / ask $53,892; our 03:37 sample gave $53,287 / $53,225.
  • Not a collector artifact: in the same sampling run only one of 15 books we sample on Binance (SAPIEN) rose more than 1.8×. The collector runs before and after completed normally, with their usual few unread pairs. The step also shows on the exchanges' own book timestamps.
  • Not truncated: no sample of these books was flagged as cut off inside ±2% of mid in the window.

Reproduce it

Queries: queries.sql (ClickHouse, table defi_chain.cex_depth_samples). The tab-separated files above are their outputs.

What this does and does not say

  • It says the measured depth near the price differed sharply between exchanges for the same token, and when it changed.
  • It does not say who provided the liquidity, why it changed, or that anyone acted in concert. A public book is the total of every maker on the pair.
  • It makes no claim about SAPIEN's price direction; the price traded between about $0.036 and $0.048 in the window.
  • Historical figures are WATAM's own samples. Only today's books could be checked against an exchange API.
