What it measures

One check takes a network, a token address and an amount (in tokens or in USD) and returns:

  • Displayed value: the amount times a reference price. This is what a wallet or portfolio tracker shows.
  • You would receive: the best sell quote among the sources that answered, paid in the network's main stablecoin.
  • Shortfall: how far the quote is below the displayed value.
  • Price impact: how much worse the price for your full amount is than the price for a small 1 % probe, from the same source.
  • Route: the venues and pools the best quote would use and how the amount splits between them.
  • Flags: only what can be computed, such as no route found, sources that disagree by more than 5 %, one source answering alone, or a large shortfall.
  • Verified exit depth (catalog tokens): how many tokens it takes to move the price of the exit pools by 2, 5 and 10 %, from our own chain reads.

Each number carries a label: estimated (from a price or a quote) or verified (from our chain data). Every check gets a short result link you can share; the link expires after a while.

Why displayed value is not exit value

A last price describes the most recent small trade. It says nothing about what happens when you sell a position into the pools that actually hold the liquidity. A larger sale walks down the pool's price curve, is split across venues, and can be priced by market makers that quote only some sizes.

What a wallet showsBalance × last price. The same number whether you hold $500 or $500,000 of the token.
What Exit Check showsWhat executable quotes pay for your amount now, and how deep the pools are around it.

For teams this is the difference between a treasury marked at last price and a treasury you could actually turn into stablecoins. For holders and listing or risk desks it is a quick size-versus-liquidity sanity check before a decision.

What “shortfall” means

Shortfall is receive ÷ displayed − 1. If the displayed value is $10,000 and the best quote pays $9,200, the shortfall is −8 %. It bundles everything between a last price and a sale: price impact, venue fees and spread. It is a snapshot of one moment, not a prediction, and it is not a guarantee that a sale at that size would succeed. A source that cannot answer is shown with its status and is never replaced by a number.

Reference priceA current price from a public price feed with a confidence and age check; a price that fails the check is not used.
Verified depth (catalog tokens)Our own on-chain reads of the exit pools' state at one block, walked through the pool math at −2, −5 and −10 %, with the share of depth that sits in verified pools. Method: how data is collected and verified.

Quotes are requested only when you submit a check, shared briefly between visitors and rate limited per source. When a source is busy it is skipped and the result says so; that is never reported as “no route”.

Limits and disclaimer

  • Informational only, not investment advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold.
  • Quotes are indicative. They change every block, are not an order, and include no gas, tax or slippage setting. Your actual sale can differ.
  • Not checked in this version: sell tax or honeypot behaviour and liquidity lock or LP ownership. A quote with a route does not prove that a sale will succeed or that no tax applies; each result states this.
  • Catalog depth covers verified pools only. It describes the pools we measured, never a token's whole market, and the result shows what share of the depth is verified.
  • Provider availability can change. Public quote endpoints may be rate limited or unavailable without notice. Independent analytics; not affiliated with the projects, exchanges or providers named.
