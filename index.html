Every finding answers five questions

Each anomaly finding comes with an Evidence Card you can hand to your own team, auditor or decision maker. The application, the API and every report export show the same five sections, built only from stored data. A value we cannot determine is shown as unknown, with the reason, never guessed.

  • Less research time: the pools, blocks and largest transactions are already gathered.
  • Quality checks first: coverage and gaps are part of the answer.
  • Reproducible: anyone can rerun it without access to our database.
Evidence Cardevidence/1.0
1What was observed?

A plain-language statement of the anomaly: the metric and its unit, the observed value against the expected baseline, and the window in UTC.

Types
Volume, swap count and liquidity shifts, buy/sell imbalance, concentration and more
Window
Start and end in UTC, end exclusive
2Where is the evidence?

On-chain, with explorer links.

Subject
Network, chain id and the token's contract
Blocks
The block range of the window's decoded swaps
Pools
Each measured pool with its protocol and decoder family
Transactions
Swap count and the five largest transactions
Sources
The RPC providers and the ingestion runs covering the window
3How was it computed?

The method and its version, the calculation version, the baseline window and value, the parameters and the detector's facts. The baseline is frozen when the anomaly starts, so it cannot drift towards the spike it measures.

4How much can we trust it?

Data quality and evidence strength, stated next to the finding rather than in a footnote.

Coverage
Hours expected against hours fully covered by successful runs, and every gap
Checks
Token identity, ingestion gates, data-quality audit, decoder family, pool identity
Caveats
Limitations, alternative explanations and uncertainties
5How can it be checked independently?

Ordered, self-contained steps: our detection SQL bound to its parameters with its SHA-256, and a recipe for any public JSON-RPC node that needs no access to our database.

From a raw log to a finding you can check

Each step leaves evidence behind. Nothing reaches a report unless the step before it can be reproduced.

  1. Ingest

    Logs are read by block range for each network, with the RPC and range recorded for every run. Empty answers are confirmed by a second provider.

  2. Decode

    Swap, Sync and Transfer events are decoded per venue family, from recorded factories only.

  3. Gate

    A run is published only if coverage, decoding and uniqueness checks pass. Gaps are listed, never filled with zeros.

  4. Detect

    Volume, swap-count and liquidity shifts are measured against a frozen baseline, per token and pool.

  5. Explain

    Every finding ships with its Evidence Card: method, alternative explanations, the SQL and the public-node recipe.

See it on real data: ZORA on Base — a week of DEX activity, checked on a public node.

What you get without building it

The work between raw chain data and an answer you can defend is the expensive part. That is the part we do.

  • Shorter research

    A finding arrives with its pools, block range, largest transactions and method, so your analyst starts from the evidence instead of collecting it.

  • Quality checks before numbers

    Coverage, decoding and uniqueness gates run before anything is published. Swaps without a usable price stay unvalued, never counted as zero.

  • Reproducible findings

    Detection SQL with its hash, a public-node recipe, method and code versions. Your team or auditor can arrive at the same numbers without us.

  • Monitoring that keeps running

    Catalog tokens are refreshed on a schedule (EVM every 12 hours, TON every 3, Solana hourly) and audited for data quality, with anomaly alerts by email on the EVM networks.

With a query platform such as Dune you write and check the SQL yourself; with an in-house build you also run and maintain the pipeline. Here the checks, the evidence trail and the monitoring come with every finding, and Dune remains an optional cross-check.
