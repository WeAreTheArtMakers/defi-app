Reported Binance hot-wallet outflow, 29 October 2026: DEX data note

An independent data note. It describes what our verified DEX swap data for tracked tokens shows around the reported Binance hot-wallet outflow of 29 October 2026. It is not a forensic trace, makes no accusation, and makes no statement about causes, customer impact or recovery of funds.

What was reported

  • At about 20:24 UTC on 29 October 2026 a hot wallet labelled “Binance 14” was reported to have sent about 167.5k ETH, about 1.6 billion USDT and more than 60 other tokens (reported examples: RLUSD, wBETH, XAUt, UNI, USD1) to a newly created address in one transaction. (main transaction, block 26,300,770)
  • A later transfer of about 780 million USDC was reported. Reported totals differ between outlets, from about $2.5 billion upward.
  • Reports say the address that initiated the transaction had been funded through Tornado Cash; this comes from a single researcher's alert and was reported as not independently confirmed.
  • Tether was reported to have frozen about 971 million USDT and Circle about 823 million USDC; other reports give different totals.
  • Binance was reported to be investigating; statements quoted in the reports said user funds were safe. At the time of these reports Binance had not formally confirmed the cause.
  • On-chain, the reported receiving address sent 7,018,792 UNI to address 0x42cd…7810 in block 26,301,181. Public on-chain research reported that this address then sold about 700k UNI into Uniswap pools between blocks 26,301,203 and 26,301,244; our own observation of tracked pools is in the table below. (UNI transfer) (address 0x42cd…7810)

Reported by: PANews, BeInCrypto, Cointelegraph, Crypto Briefing (stablecoin freezes), Crypto Briefing (funding report), Crypto Briefing (statements), Blockchain Reporter. These facts are reported by third parties and are not measured by us; figures differ between outlets and may change.

Method

  • Data: Swap events of each tracked token's verified pools (Uniswap v2-like, v3-like and v4 families), read with eth_getLogs from free public Ethereum RPC endpoints and decoded once per transaction and log index.
  • Coverage: an hour counts only when successful ingestion runs cover all of it. Uncovered hours are missing data, never zero, and are shown as “not covered yet”.
  • Baseline: median of the covered hours in the 24 h before the window (the anomaly engine's lookback) and in the 7 days before it, for swaps per hour and token volume per hour.
  • Findings: verified anomaly findings whose observation window overlaps the incident window. A finding is a statistical change in the measured pools; it does not identify a cause.
  • Reported addresses: for every transaction with a swap in a tracked pool inside covered hours, the transaction's origin (its from address) is read with eth_getTransactionByHash from a public node. A swap counts as observed only when that origin is a reported address. Swaps filled by a solver or relayer on someone's behalf are sent by the solver, so they are “not attributed” even when the tokens came from a reported address.

How to verify

Anyone can re-read the same logs from a public Ethereum JSON-RPC node, without access to our database. Block range of the window: not covered yet.

// Swap logs of the tracked pools in the window; to test one address, put it (left-padded to 32 bytes)
// in the sender or recipient topic position, or leave the position null for all swaps
{"method": "eth_getLogs", "params": [{
  "address":   ["<tracked pools of the token (JSON data file)>"],
  "topics":    ["<Swap topic>", "<topic1 or null>", "<topic2 or null>"],
  "fromBlock": "<first block of the window, hex>",
  "toBlock":   "<last block of the window, hex>"
}]}

To check who sent a listed transaction: {"method": "eth_getTransactionByHash", "params": ["<tx hash>"]} returns its from address.
